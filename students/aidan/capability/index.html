PLAN · Storage Allocation Planning

Know where your grain will fit before it arrives.

Minnesota Corn & Soybean Storage is developing a digital planning process designed to assign incoming corn and soybeans to available storage capacity within 30 minutes.

The performance goal

Digital planning can reduce the time needed to determine where grain should be stored. MHP, a Ukrainian agricultural company, reported faster grain allocation planning after adopting Microsoft Azure.

2 hours MHP before Azure
15 min MHP after Azure
30 min MCSS proposed target

Vendor claim: The MHP figures are company-reported results published by Microsoft and have not been independently verified. The 30-minute MCSS figure is our proposed target, not a measured result.

How it works

This capability improves the flow of information, helping staff make better decisions about the physical movement of grain.

1. Capture operating data

Record expected deliveries, crop type, estimated bushels, delivery timing, current inventory, available bin capacity, and storage requirements.

2. Evaluate storage options

A digital tool compares incoming grain with available capacity and identifies suitable storage locations.

3. Review and assign

The storage planner or facility manager reviews recommendations, resolves conflicts, and confirms assignments.

4. Keep information current

Staff update inventory and capacity as grain arrives or leaves, allowing future recommendations to reflect current conditions.

Real-world example: MHP

MHP uses Microsoft Azure, Power BI, and mathematical modeling to allocate approximately 2 million tons of grain and oilseeds across storage locations. Its planning considers storage capacity, field proximity, processing needs, and logistics.

MHP reported that this planning task took two hours on its own servers and takes 15 minutes on Azure. Before the tool, it took nearly two weeks and 15 people.

Source: Microsoft customer story, February 7, 2024 (vendor-published; results are reported by MHP and Microsoft, not independently verified). Read the MHP case study.

The trade-off: data accuracy

Digital recommendations depend on accurate inventory, bin capacity, delivery estimates, and grain quality information. Outdated or incorrect data can lead to poor storage assignments.

MCSS would manage this risk through regular inventory updates, capacity checks, and staff review before confirming allocations.
